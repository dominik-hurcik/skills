# Slovak rules that shape B2B contracts

Each rule below was read in the official consolidated text on 16 October 2026. See `sources.md` for the versions. Statute quotes are in Slovak, followed by an English summary. Items marked "practice note" are not statute text and were not verified.

## When the Commercial Code applies (ObZ)

- **§ 261, ods. 1.** Part three of the Commercial Code governs obligations between entrepreneurs, if it's clear when the obligation arises that it concerns their business activity.
- **§ 262.** Parties to a relationship outside § 261 can agree that the Commercial Code governs it. That agreement must be in writing.
- **§ 263, ods. 1.** Parties can depart from part three, except for a listed set of mandatory sections. The list includes §§ 301, 369 to 369d, and 386 to 408. In practice that covers penalty reduction, late interest, the ban on waiving damages, and the whole limitation regime.
- **§ 263, ods. 2.** Parties can't depart from the basic provisions of each contract type, or from rules that require written form.
- **§ 265.** "Výkon práva, ktorý je v rozpore so zásadami poctivého obchodného styku, nepožíva právnu ochranu." A party that exercises a right against the principles of fair business dealing gets no legal protection.

## Form and terms

- **§ 272, ods. 1.** A contract needs written form only where a law requires it, or where at least one party says during negotiations that it wants written form.
- **§ 272, ods. 2.** If a written contract says it can be changed or ended only in writing, written form is required for those changes.
- **§ 273.** Part of a contract can be set by reference to general business terms, if both parties know the terms or the terms are attached to the offer. Terms written in the contract itself win over the referenced terms.

## Business details on documents (ObZ § 3a)

- **§ 3a, ods. 1 and 3.** Every entrepreneur must state its business name, registered seat, legal form, and identification number (IČO) on business documents and on its website. A registered entrepreneur also states the register and the entry number.

Use in drafting. Put these details for both parties in the contract header and in invoices.

## Trade secrets (ObZ § 17)

- **§ 17, ods. 1.** A trade secret is information of a commercial, manufacturing, or technical nature related to the business. It must have actual or potential value, not be commonly available in the relevant business circles, and be meant to stay secret. The owner must also protect it in a suitable way.

Use in reviews. An NDA helps prove that the owner protects its secrets. Without protection measures, information may not count as a trade secret.

## Contractual penalties (zmluvná pokuta)

- **§ 300.** Circumstances that exclude liability (§ 374) don't remove the duty to pay a contractual penalty.
- **§ 301.** "Neprimerane vysokú zmluvnú pokutu môže súd znížiť." A court can reduce a disproportionately high penalty, down to the damage caused up to the court's decision. This section is mandatory (§ 263).
- **§ 302.** Withdrawing from the contract doesn't remove a claim to a penalty.

Use in reviews. Penalties are common and enforceable in Slovak contracts, even without fault. A disproportionate one may be cut by a court.

## Damages (ObZ §§ 373 to 386)

- **§ 373.** A party that breaches an obligation must compensate the resulting damage, unless it proves circumstances that exclude liability. Liability doesn't depend on fault.
- **§ 374, ods. 1.** A circumstance excludes liability if it arose independently of the obliged party's will, prevents performance, couldn't reasonably have been averted or overcome, and couldn't have been foreseen when the obligation arose. This is the statutory force majeure test.
- **§ 374, ods. 2.** An obstacle that arose while the party was already in delay, or that stems from its own economic situation, doesn't exclude liability.
- **§ 379.** Damages cover actual loss and lost profit. They don't cover damage beyond what the obliged party foresaw, or could have foreseen, as a possible result of breach when the obligation arose.
- **§ 386, ods. 1.** "Nároku na náhradu škody sa nemožno vzdať pred porušením povinnosti, z ktorého môže škoda vzniknúť." A party can't waive a damages claim before the breach that may cause the damage.
- **§ 386, ods. 2.** A court can't reduce damages.

**Practice note on liability caps, not verified.** Slovak lawyers debate whether a liability cap counts as a partial advance waiver under § 386, ods. 1. A common approach is to state in the contract the foreseeable amount of damage under § 379, instead of a plain cap. Get a Slovak lawyer's view before relying on any cap.

## Late payment

- **§ 369, ods. 1.** If a debtor is late with a money obligation, the creditor can claim late interest at the agreed rate, without a separate reminder.
- **§ 369, ods. 2.** If no rate was agreed, the rate set by government regulation applies.
- **Nariadenie 21/2013, § 1, ods. 1.** That rate is the European Central Bank's main refinancing rate on the first day of the relevant calendar half-year of the delay, plus 8 percentage points.
- **§ 369c and Nariadenie 21/2013, § 2.** The creditor is also entitled to a flat collection fee of 40 EUR, once per claim, regardless of the length of the delay.
- **§ 369d, ods. 1.** A term on the due date, the late-interest rate, or the flat fee that is grossly unfair to the creditor without good reason is void.
- **§ 340b.** If the debtor is a public-sector body, the payment term is 30 days, and at most 60 days in limited cases. Out of scope for private B2B deals, but flag it if the customer is public.

## Limitation periods

- **§ 397.** The general limitation period is 4 years.
- **§ 398.** For damages, the period runs from when the injured party learned, or could have learned, of the damage and who must pay. It ends at the latest 10 years after the breach.
- **§ 401.** The obliged party can extend the period by a written declaration, also before it starts. The total can't exceed 10 years.
- Sections 386 to 408 are mandatory (§ 263, ods. 1). So a contract can't shorten the limitation period.

## Copyright and software (Autorský zákon)

- **§ 18, ods. 3.** Moral rights, such as authorship and integrity of the work, can't be waived or transferred.
- **§ 20, ods. 2.** "Majetkové práva sú neprevoditeľné, nemožno sa ich vzdať a nemožno ich postihnúť exekúciou." Economic rights can't be transferred or waived. The only way to give someone rights in a work is a licence.
- **§ 65, ods. 1.** A licence agreement contains, in particular, the manner of use, the scope, the duration, and the fee, unless the licence is free.
- **§ 65, ods. 3.** An exclusive licence must be in writing.
- **§ 66.** If the manner of use isn't stated, the licence covers only the uses needed for the contract's purpose. A licence can't cover a manner of use that wasn't known when the contract was made.
- **§ 67, ods. 3.** If the scope isn't stated and doesn't follow from the purpose, the licence is limited to the territory of Slovakia.
- **§ 68, ods. 2.** If the duration isn't stated and doesn't follow from the purpose, the licence lasts for the usual time for that kind of work, and at most one year.
- **§ 70, ods. 1.** A licence is non-exclusive unless the contract says it's exclusive.
- **§ 72, ods. 1 and 2.** The licensee can grant a sublicence, or assign the licence, only with the author's prior consent. The consent isn't needed when the licence passes with the sale of a business, unless agreed otherwise.
- **§ 87.** A computer program is protected if it results from the author's creative intellectual activity. Ideas and principles behind it, including those behind its interfaces, aren't protected.
- **§ 90, ods. 4.** For a work an employee creates as part of their job, the employer exercises the economic rights in its own name, unless agreed otherwise.
- **§ 91, ods. 4.** For a computer program made wholly or partly to order, the employee-work rules apply, and the customer counts as the employer.

Use in drafting. A software development contract under Slovak law should still state the licence terms explicitly: worldwide, unlimited in time, all manners of use, exclusive if needed, with the right to modify, sublicense, and assign. Don't rely only on § 91, ods. 4. Where the supplier uses freelancers rather than employees, make sure the supplier holds the rights it licenses.
