# KTZR golden rules

The overriding principles for working with contracts. If they conflict with any other instruction in this skill, these rules win.

These rules govern the Polish contract text you draft. Polish phrases in quotation marks are the wording to use in the contract itself.

## Cardinal rules

1. **Every capitalised term MUST have a definition** in the Definitions section (§ Definicje). A missing definition is a defect in the contract.

2. **Consistent terminology.** One concept, one term, throughout the document. Don't mix "Wykonawca", "Zleceniobiorca", and "Usługodawca" in one contract.

3. **Every internal cross-reference** (§ X ust. Y, art. Z) must point to a provision that exists. Always check cross-references after an edit.

4. **No orphaned annexes.** Every annex named in the preamble or the body must exist and be referred to. A missing annex, or an annex nobody refers to, is a defect.

5. **No repetition.** One rule, one place in the contract. If the penalty clause is in § 8, don't repeat it in § 12 "Postanowienia końcowe" (final provisions).

6. **Never trade legal precision for brevity.** Brevity is a bonus. Precision is mandatory.

7. **Map the essentialia negotii BEFORE you start**: contract type, parties, subject matter, remuneration, and term. Don't write a single paragraph before they're mapped.

8. **Every contract must have** a date, a place of signing, and how each party is represented, with the basis of authority (KRS entry or power of attorney).

9. **Protective clauses always favour the client you work for**, unless the user explicitly says otherwise (for example, "we're drafting for the other side, check how they protect us").

10. **Contract language.** Formal, precise, and understandable. **No Latin in the clause text** (essentialia negotii, lucrum cessans, dolus, culpa, ex contractu: there are ZERO in the clause bank). Latin belongs in legal commentary and internal analysis, not in the contract people sign. Exception: settled Polish legal expressions ("siła wyższa", "z mocy prawa") where leaving them out would change the meaning.

11. **Definitions first, references after (the DRY rule).** Define every term that needs a definition (see rule 1) **once, at the start of the contract**: in § Definicje, or in the preamble as *„(dalej: „Pojęcie")"*. In the rest of the contract, use only the defined term. Never repeat the definition.

    **Exceptions.** A *locally scoped* definition is fine when a term matters only inside one paragraph (for example *„Okres Przejściowy"* defined in § 2 of an image-rights settlement, and used only in §§ 2 to 4). The principle is one place of definition, many places of use.

    **Benefit.** Changing a definition in one place updates every clause that uses it (see also `clause-bank/02-preambuly.md`, the section "Technika: definicja jako zbiór zamknięty").

12. **§ 1 is always the Subject of the Contract: what the deal is, in one paragraph.** Every contract, whatever its type, starts its substantive part with § 1 „Przedmiot Umowy" (or the equivalent: „Przedmiot Ugody", „Przedmiot Porozumienia"). § 1 answers *what the Parties are agreeing to*, without operational detail.

    **For simple contracts** (NDA, assignment, one-off engagement, short settlement), § 1 can already contain the parties' complete obligations and be the last substantive paragraph before the final provisions.

    **For complex contracts** (IT body leasing, implementation, framework agreement, comprehensive settlement), § 1 is **a map of the contract**. It lists, point by point, what the contract governs, and later paragraphs give the details.

    **Pattern for a complex contract:**
    > *„§ 1. Przedmiot Umowy*
    > *Przedmiotem Umowy jest: 1) [zobowiązanie 1, doprecyzowane w § 2]; 2) [zobowiązanie 2, doprecyzowane w § 3]; 3) [zobowiązanie 3, doprecyzowane w § 4]."*

    **Anti-pattern.** A § 1 full of detailed operational obligations that belong in separate paragraphs: deadlines, remuneration, sanctions, performance mechanics. § 1 is a map, not a book.

    **Drafting test.** After writing § 1, ask yourself: *would someone who reads only § 1 know what the Parties are agreeing to?* If yes, § 1 does its job. Operational detail comes later.

## The overriding golden rule

> **A contract must protect the client's interests as well as possible, BUT it must be acceptable to the other side.**

If you propose a clause that will certainly be rejected in negotiations (for example unlimited liability for the counterparty, penalties of 100% of the contract value, or a 10-year non-compete without compensation), label it "aggressive variant" and also give a negotiable variant.

## Style guidelines for Polish clause text

- Use the future-obligation form „zobowiązuje się do" instead of „będzie zobowiązany".
- Use „Strony zgodnie oświadczają" or „Strony postanawiają" instead of „Strony deklarują".
- Write amounts in figures and in words („10 000 PLN (słownie: dziesięć tysięcy złotych)"), but only for remuneration and penalties. Always write dates in figures.
- Capitalise party names: **Zleceniodawca**, **Wykonawca**. No italics in the body.
- Put a term in quotation marks at first use, with its definition („Specjalista"). After that, write just Specjalista.
